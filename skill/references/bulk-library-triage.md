# Bulk triage of the whole image library

Screening a library before a review round, and the show bar that decides what may be shown at all. A whole generation series can share one broken prompt template, so most of the list is derivable without looking at a single picture.

## The show bar

An image reaches a review card only if it passes all four clauses. This outranks the older round-1 habit of showing a draft's broken picture so a rejection costs him one click.

1. **The figure is correct** — two hands, two arms, one head, one person unless the prompt says otherwise. No three hands, no missing limb, no body that reads as broken. A count sentence in the prompt is not the count in the frame, so count heads on a read of the whole frame: a second copy of the same figure is drawn behind a large prop that could swallow a body (a crate, a bin, a pile of sheets) even when the prompt named one man, and it sits cropped by the prop or the frame edge, where it reads as scenery until it is counted. **A glossy surface beside the figure is the other way a second copy appears:** an open brushed-steel door, a mirror, a window over dark glass draws the man again inside it, and two readers will then agree the frame holds two figures while disagreeing about whether the second is a reflection or a second man. The count is the finding and the interpretation is not — two copies of the man is a hold-back whatever the second one is called, so the note says "two figures, the second inside the open door" instead of arguing about reflections.
2. **No colourful artifacts** — no flat panel, badge, tile, sticker, banner, notification card or swatch strip pasted over the painted scene; nothing drawn in a different idiom from the frame; no photograph with flat vector graphics laid over it.
3. **Text only if it is readable English** — `Racoiving`, `HelmRelest`, `H16`, `- 156` are defects, not quirks. A picture with no lettering at all is fine.
4. **The beat reads** — the prop the section names is present, the action points the right way, and the frame is one continuous scene.

A render that fails gets one silent re-render inside the same round, then the slot waits. It does not go on the card with the defect named.

**The bar outranks a card description that says round 1 renders nothing.** A backlog-review card's own description carries the parent's older line — this is not an image request, do not render anything for round 1 except a missing image — and that line governs only a draft whose images already pass. Read every draft PNG against the four clauses before opening the card: when one fails, the slot is re-rendered as an ideogram + flux pair and the broken draft PNG never reaches the card, with the opening comment naming the slot, the defect and the job ids. Holding the check behind "round 1 renders nothing" is the habit he retired.

## Repair the prompt by removal, not by ban

- Drop the `color_palette` array — it renders as the swatch strip and nothing else depends on it.
- Delete literal marks (a red X, a green checkmark) and show the state instead: a bent part against a straight one, a dead lamp against a lit one.
- Take the text-inviting prop out of the hands — clipboard, label, plaque, screen, tablet, board, nameplate — and replace it with a plain object.
- **When the beat itself is the screen, the screen goes, not just its text.** "A monitor showing the list with two entries at the top" cannot be repaired by asking for a blank screen: a screen surface is what the model writes on, and it comes back full of pseudo-text whatever the prompt says. Carry the beat with a physical arrangement in the same order the list had — a vertical board or rail of identical plain objects with the newest two clipped at the very top and the rest dusted over — and say what the objects are made of and that their faces are blank. The prop carries the story; the monitor, if the room needs one, is turned side-on with its face away and dark.
- State the cast and the limbs, describe a prop in every corner, and end the description with the negative clause naming the shapes to keep out.
- **Name each limb and what it is doing, not just "two arms".** A stump or a third limb growing from the torso is drawn from an under-specified body, and it survives a ban on extra limbs: state the cast as a count plus a pose — "exactly two arms and two hands: his right arm is raised holding X, his left arm hangs down at his side with the hand on his knee, nothing else grows out of his body". The pose sentence is what removes the stump, because the model has no room left to invent an arm.
- **Describe every surface matte when a glossy prop stands beside the figure.** A steel cabinet door swung open, a window or a polished panel reflects him, and the render puts a second copy of the man inside it. The repair is not a ban on reflections — that hands the model a synonym to draw — but a scene where nothing can mirror: "every surface in the room is matte: bare plaster, painted steel, unwaxed wood", the doors "folded flat back against the cabinet's own sides as plain scuffed panels", and the cast stated as "he is the only person in the room and he appears once".
- **A shape mismatch has to be described hole by hole.** "The wrong part is in the slot" gives a tidy row of identical inserts and the beat is gone. Name the shapes in order and say where the empty space is: three round holes each holding a round disc flush, and one square hole holding a round disc, "so the four corners of the square hole stand open and empty around it". The empty corners ARE the picture; a part that fits hides the fault.

## The sweep

`scripts/audit-assets.py` walks every PNG under `tech_blog/assets/`, runs `check-grey-bars.py` and `check-palette-strip.py` on each, and writes a JSONL row per asset plus a per-post markdown table of the failures.

```bash
PY=/Users/paperclip/image-studio/.venv-imgcheck/bin/python
$PY ~/.hermes/profiles/matejko/skills/image-studio/scripts/audit-assets.py \
   --out ~/image-studio/backlog/audit/<run>-sweep.jsonl \
   --table ~/image-studio/backlog/audit/<run>-sweep-table.md
```

It prints a progress line every fifty frames and flushes each row, so a killed run still leaves a usable partial table. Keep both files: the table is what the next batch reads, the JSONL is what a later run re-derives it from without re-scanning.

## Joining an asset to its post

The library record is the join key, not the filename. Each record's frontmatter carries `asset:` — but it is spelled three ways across the library (`tech_blog/assets/2026-09-26/x.png`, `tech_blog/…`, `assets/…`) while a filesystem walk yields `2026-09-26/x.png`. **Strip every prefix (`tech_blog/assets/`, `tech_blog/`, `assets/`) before comparing, or the join silently matches nothing and the report says zero defects on a library full of them.** A record whose `superseded_by` is `null` is the live one for its slot; a superseded record points at an image the draft no longer carries, so count those separately rather than as work. **A superseded record is not closed by `superseded_by` alone.** Setting the pointer leaves the frontmatter at `verdict: "unreviewed"` with the back-fill line still under the Verdicts header, so the library reads as an image nobody judged while the draft has already moved past it — the tell is a pointer set beside an unreviewed verdict. Close it with the same three edits an approval makes (`scripts/approve_record.py <record> --rejected --issue <GUL-x> --reason "never shown: <the defect that kept it off a card>, replaced by <job id>"`): the verdict, one row, and the back-fill line deleted. Do it in the run that applied the approval, because the round that owned that record has usually closed by then and nobody else is coming for it.

## What the sweep can and cannot see

- **Can settle, no model needed:** grey bars (an empty band at the top or bottom) and a rendered `color_palette` strip along an edge. **A strip is only half-settled by the script, so crop the band it reports (its row range, full width, upscaled) and read it:** the model sometimes signs the strip, so one chip carries a word while its neighbours stay flat, and that strip fails clause 2 and clause 3 at once because it is a badge as much as a palette array. Quote the word verbatim in the check note beside the row range and the chip widths the script printed. **The rendered array does not always come back as colour chips.** A batch whose prompts carried a `color_palette` array plus a screen, label or calendar surface comes back with the array as a **dark caption bar across the bottom edge holding a whole pseudo-English sentence** — read by a full-frame pass as a letterbox border or a painted plinth — or as a floating UI panel with doodle arithmetic over the scene. Both are the same finding: `check-palette-strip.py` reports the band at flat-column percentages in the fifties with several hue groups, and the lettering is legible only on the upscaled crop of the reported rows, so crop and read it character by character before the note names the class. It fails clause 2 and clause 3 at once, and the repair is the array **and** the text-bearing prop, because deleting one of the two leaves the other to reproduce the bar. Depth is not a defence — a strip fifty rows deep on an 832-row frame is still the palette array rendered, and at that depth it looks like a border to a full-frame read. **The colour test does not clear a strip: a band whose hues also appear on props in the scene is still the array rendered.** SKILL.md step 6 lets a band whose colour matches a palette entry and is also painted on a described prop count as a prop, and that test fails on any scene with a strong house palette — a workshop with a blue machine, warm amber lamp light and a grey steel cabinet carries three of a six-entry array's hues in scenery, so every chip of the strip finds a prop to hide behind and the false clearance is easy to write down. Judge the shape instead: one row at the very edge, equal-width hard-edged blocks with no shading or perspective, and `check-palette-strip.py` reading 80%+ flat columns against a single-digit control band. A band with that shape is an artifact however many of its colours the scene happens to use, and the colour argument belongs in the note only as the weaker second piece of evidence.
- **Cannot settle:** three hands, a pasted panel with invented lettering, a photograph with vector graphics over it, a beat pointing the wrong way. Those need a read of the frame, or a crop on disk when one prop decides it.
- **Two readers on one disk crop settle a pasted-panel claim.** `vision_analyze` on a `crop-probe.py` crop, then `scripts/vread.py` on the same box with the same narrow question ("is there a flat UI card pasted over the scene; read its text verbatim"), returned the identical garbled strings twice on each of three images of one post — that agreement is the evidence, and one read is not. `check-defect-signature.py` corroborates it numerically: the two blue panels carried `blue_px` 154,465 and 129,861 against 12,092 on the same series' header, so a panel's own colour is where the count spikes.
- **When the two readers disagree about a count, the question was the defect, not the picture.** One reader called a 2x crop "three arms, a hand at the chest" on the same file where the other called it "2 hands, 2 arms, all traceable", and a question that offers the defect ("is there an extra arm?") gets the defect confirmed. Settle it in this order: re-ask **both** readers the same neutral wording on the **same written crop** ("how many hands, and can every arm be traced from a shoulder to its hand"); then crop the **seat** of the alleged extra limb at 6x and count there, because two hands side by side at 2x is one hand at 6x and what the model filled was the gap between a bent forearm and the prop's edge; then write the count both readers agree on into the note. A count you did not settle never goes on the card as a caveat.
- **A disagreement about WHAT a mark reads is not a disagreement about whether the defect exists.** Two readers gave the same flat decal as `C3` and as `83` on the same crop; the string is unsettleable and irrelevant, because clause 3 asks only whether the lettering is readable English and neither reading is. Do not spend a third read chasing the exact glyphs and do not soften the finding to "unclear": quote both readings in the note and name the class. The same asymmetry runs the other way for a count — there the number IS the finding, so it must be settled before it goes on a card.
- **Attached to a wall is not the same as diegetic.** A flat shape with uniform fill, hard edges, no perspective and no cast shadow is a pasted sticker whether it floats in mid-air or sits flush on the painted wall, and a reader will call a wall-mounted one "a decal that belongs in the room". The idiom test decides, not the contact: if the frame's own drawing gives it no thickness, no light and no shadow, it is an overlay and the frame fails clause 2.
- **`check-flat-bands.py` is never part of the sweep's verdict.** It fires on virtually every cel-shaded frame — a full sweep returned a hit on all 566 assets — because flat colour blocks are the art style. It is a prompt to crop and look.
- **A keyword grep over the prompts is not a defect list either.** Counting `color_palette`, mark words and text-prop words across every record flags approved images at the same rate as broken ones, because a prompt that *bans* lettering contains the same nouns as one that asks for it. Use the prompt scan on a slot you are already looking at, and let the pixels decide.

## Reading the result

Group by post, worst first, and put every row into the repair queue with its slot id and record path. A frame that fails grey bars or a palette strip is a re-render, full stop — those two are the failures that never turn out to be intentional.

## Pace it

At most six renders per run (three slots × two engines), and one batch per run. Do not empty the board at once: withdrawing every open card means re-rendering everything behind them while the worker is already hours deep. Take the posts whose card sits closest to the review front, and say plainly in the issue comment which posts you did not touch and why — a decision he can overrule, stated where he reads it.

**When every slot of a draft fails, the slot is released at submit time, not held until the render lands.** A round-1 card whose whole image set fails the bar becomes a render round with no card, and the queue's single slot token is what every other post's card is waiting for: held for a deep-queue render it stops the entire backlog for as long as the queue takes, which on a shared worker has been most of a day. So `backlog.py note <n>` the failures and job ids, `backlog.py card-done <n> --rendering`, arm the monitor, and Advance; the renders' card is queued from `ready` at the collect wake and is still the post's round 1. The exact order, the brief's wording for a card that shows nothing already in the draft, and the `ready` call are in `references/review-loop.md` §4 — follow that rather than inventing a slot-holding variant because no card was ever shown.

**A partial failure is the other case: hold the slot, but only while the last render is inside the hour.** When at least one slot of the draft passes the bar, the round still owes one card that carries every slot, so the passing image waits instead of going up alone — two cards for one post is what the one-card rule forbids, and a card with a third of the post on it is not the review the brief asked for. Gate the hold on the queue's own numbers: read `imagegen status <last job>` for the ETA, hold when it is under about an hour (two renders per failed slot, FLUX submitted first, usually lands inside it on a shallow queue), and release with `card-done <n> --rendering` plus the standard monitor hop when it is longer, because a slot held for a night stalls every other post's card. Either way the note and the opening comment name which slots failed the bar, why, and the job ids, so the round reads the same whether the card comes now or at the collect wake.

## Render pairs and the queue

- Every regeneration goes twice: `--engine ideogram4` (main) and `--engine flux2-klein` (comparison), both items on the same card so he can compare per slot. FLUX also exposes a bad *concept* in three minutes, before 21 minutes go on the Ideogram render. The brief has an id only for the slot, so the ideogram keeps it and the companion takes `<slot>-flux` as its own item id and label, with its own attachment and its own description — one row per render on the card, the slot's record and asset chain following the ideogram whichever one he approves. **The two submits take different flags:** `imagegen submit --prompt-json <file>.json --engine ideogram4 --name <slot>-r1` for the main render, and `imagegen submit --prompt-file <file>.txt --engine flux2-klein --name <slot>-r1-flux` for the companion. Flux takes its natural-language text through `--prompt-file` (or `--prompt`); `--prompt-json` is the ideogram4 structured prompt and is not the flag for a `.txt`.
- Both engines share **one FIFO queue ordered by submit time, not by engine speed**. Submit the FLUX jobs first so they lead the batch, and compute the monitor from the *last* Ideogram's ETA plus five minutes. `queue_ahead` counts every pending job regardless of engine, so a 'fast' engine submitted behind fifteen Ideograms still waits hours.
- **The renderer's queue is shared with every sibling run, so the ETA you read is the queue's, not your job's.** When several card runs render in the same hour the queue can sit seventy-odd jobs deep and the last job's `eta_minutes` is a night away, not twenty-one minutes. Read it before promising anything: `imagegen status <last-job-id>` prints `queue_ahead` and `eta_minutes`, and those two numbers are the whole wait.
- **Size the queue from its own database, not from your job's number, when the answer changes the plan.** The jobs live on the generation host, and the local `~/image-studio/jobs.db` is not them:

  ```bash
  ssh m1max 'cd ~/git/ideogram4-local && python3 -c "
  import sqlite3; c=sqlite3.connect(\"jobs.db\"); cur=c.cursor()
  for r in cur.execute(\"SELECT status,engine,count(*) FROM jobs GROUP BY status,engine\"): print(r)"
  '
  ```

  The table is `jobs` and the state column is **`status`**, not `state` (`pending`, `running`, `done`, `failed`). It tells you whether your render is behind forty Ideograms or whether the queue is shallow and the worker stalled — two different problems with two different answers.
- **A long ETA is handled with short monitor hops, because one long `nextCheckAt` is silently dropped.** A monitor set many hours out does not come back at all; **two-hour hops persist**. So arm the issue monitor at now + 2h, and on every wake re-read `imagegen status` for each job and re-arm for another 2h until the jobs are `done`. Say in the issue comment that the watch is a hop chain and why, so the long wait does not read as a forgotten card.
- **A pair is not all-or-nothing, so one held-back companion does not hold the card.** When the main render passes and the companion fails the show bar, post the card with the items that pass and name the missing companion in `detailsMarkdown` with its job id and the class that sank it. The slot's image is the deliverable and the companion is a comparison; parking five good renders for hours behind one cheap draft costs him a review day for nothing. Re-render the companion under the same slot with a `b` suffix (`<item id>-r1b-flux`) and keep it as the spare that rides the next round if he rejects the main render. Do not put the failed attempt on the card with its defect named. **This is the bar-failure case and it is not the deviation case:** a render that passes the bar and merely departs from the prompt in one prop ships with that departure named on the card, because his eye overrules your read. A render that fails a bar clause (a stump limb, invented lettering, a lost beat) never reaches a card at all, whatever it cost.
- **When the main render fails and the companion passes, the passing companion does not rescue the slot.** The ideogram is the engine that ships, so a companion that clears the bar still leaves the slot with no shippable image and the slot waits. Re-render the **ideogram only**, once, under the same round with a `b` suffix (`<item id>-r1b`), and keep the already-fetched companion as the card's comparison item rather than spending a second flux render on a picture that already passed. The card's item id stays the slot's round id (`<slot>-r1`) with the attempt suffix on the render name alone, so the slot remains one row and the held-back attempt is named in that row's note by its job id. Do not promote the companion to the slot's image: a comparison render that becomes the shipped asset inverts the engine convention that every record, alt text and later round is then read against. **A render round whose corrected ideogram is hours out therefore waits, and waiting costs the queue nothing** — a post released with `card-done --rendering` holds no slot, so the only clock running is his review, not another post's card. That is what separates this case from the deep-queue rule in `references/review-loop.md` §4: there a replacement is pushed to the next round because it is holding the live slot behind it, and here nothing is. Post the card when the `b` ideogram passes — but **when the correction is hours out rather than minutes, ship the slots that already have a main render and name the waiting slot on the card face** with its job ids, its class and its ETA, instead of filling the row with the companion. Hours is the test, not a day: a shared queue refills under you, so a five-hour estimate read at submit time has turned into a night by the next wake, and a round-1 post that has never shown a card is the strongest case of all — four checked renders in front of him beat a complete round much later. The held slot then rides its own card when its render passes, and the post stays in `rendering` until that card is answered; record the split with `backlog.py note <n>` and write the draft brief so the next run inherits the paths, the hashes and the check notes rather than re-deriving them. **A clause the ideogram fails twice is the author's, not a third prompt's.** When attempt `a` and the corrected `b` that states the clause positively both come back with the same defect — one figure asked for twice and drawn twice, in two different compositions — the engine has said what it will do, and a third attempt spends a render on a coin already flipped twice. If the companion passes the bar it then goes up **as the slot's candidate**: one row, and one clause on its face saying that approving it makes this flux frame the picture for that embed, so the verdict cannot be mistaken for a comparison pick. The engine convention bends only when the card says so in the open — what the rule forbids is the *silent* promotion, an approved flux asset that no reader was told about. When he rejects a disclosed companion, the slot goes to the post's author for a composition (`references/re-render-defect-persistence.md`), because the defect is the scene and the wording has already been tried. **Name the item for the round its card carries, not the round the render was made in:** a slot that first reaches a card on the post's round 2 takes `<slot>-r2-flux` as its item id and asset stem while its staging file is still `<slot>-r1-flux-<token>.png`; the brief carries the staging stem, the record follows the fetched stem, and the row carries the job id, so none of the three has to be inferred from another.
- **Never clear, cancel or delete queued jobs to shorten the wait — they belong to sibling runs.** The queue depth is a fact to report and plan around, not a misconfiguration to fix. **A render whose slot got approved by another picture stays queued and uncollected, and that is the correct end state:** name it in the closing comment and the queue note as a spare attempt with its job id, so a later reader does not read an orphaned job as a lost render. Collect it only if he rejects the approved picture it belongs beside.
