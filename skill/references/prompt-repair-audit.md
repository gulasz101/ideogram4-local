# Auditing and repairing a backlog of existing images

How to work a whole backlog of drafts whose images were made from an older prompt template, when the owner says *only show me the ones that are already correct*.

This is the batch counterpart to the per-slot re-render rules in `references/re-render-defect-persistence.md`. The per-slot rules answer "this image was rejected, now what" — this file answers "there are a hundred of these, where do I start and what do I show".

## The standing gate: a broken image never reaches a card

An image may be shown for review only if all four hold:

1. **The figure is correct** — two hands, two arms, one head, one person unless the prompt asked for more.
2. **No colourful artifacts** — no flat panel, badge, tile, sticker, banner, notification card or swatch strip pasted over the painted scene; nothing in an idiom that does not belong to the frame; no photograph with vector graphics laid on it.
3. **Text only if it is readable English** — a misspelt or invented string (`Racoiving`, `HelmRelest`, `H16`, `- 156`) is a defect however neatly it is drawn. No lettering at all is fine. **Correct spelling does not buy a pass:** a flat banner or name card pasted over the scene can carry perfect English (`Durable network storage`, `Local disk`) and still fails clause 2, because the panel is the defect and the words only say which kind it is. Judge the two clauses separately — well-spelled text on a flat rectangle is evidence the model was handed a text surface, never evidence the picture is shippable.
4. **The section's beat reads** — the prop the section names is there and the action points the way the section says.
5. **Nothing is blank** — no flat untextured band across an edge, no empty field filling part of the frame. Measure it rather than eyeballing it: `check-blank-margin.py` prints the blank strip running in from each of the four edges plus the dominant fill's share, and a strip on **one edge only** is the shape that reads as a picture cut short. The figure, the props and the beat can all be right while a 200-pixel cream band sits under the bench, and that render is held back like any other.

A slot that fails is **not shown**. It gets a repaired prompt and a re-render. Refining an image that fails the gate spends the owner's attention on a picture you already know is wrong, which is the loop he is asking you to stop — and his rejection is not more information than your own read at that point.

The one exception is a draft image that has never been reviewed at all and whose slot cannot be re-rendered: show it with the defect and its class rate named on the card face, because a rejection costs him one click and hands you his reason. The gate governs what you *submit*; it does not license hiding a picture he should decide on.

**A review card that wakes you for a post whose whole set fails the gate is a repair run, not a review run.** The card issue is already open and holds the queue slot, so nothing is created and nothing is shown: repair every slot, submit the engine pairs, `backlog.py card-done <post> --rendering`, `Advance` the slot to the next post, and end the run on a real monitor. The comment says the card was held back and that the draft PNGs are untouched, because the owner may want to overrule a hold-back and needs to know nothing was destroyed. Write the next round's brief as `<post>-round-<k>.draft.md` at submit time with the job ids and prompt files already in it, so the collect wake only fills in the fetched paths, sizes, hashes and check notes. **When the two engines land on different wakes, each wake appends its own settled notes to that draft.** A flux trio that finishes hours before the ideograms is fetched and checked by a run that will never see the collect, so writing its staging paths, byte sizes, sha256s and check notes into the draft as it fetches them is what stops the collect wake re-reading three files it has no reason to distrust — the draft is the only object both runs can read, and the issue comment is where the owner reads the outcome, not the data. **A hold-back does not advance the round number:** no card was ever shown for that round, so the replacements replace round 1's draft images and the card that carries them is still round 1, with the attempt suffix on the render name and the item id (`<slot>-r1`, `<slot>-r1-flux`) to tell them apart from the draft renders they displace. Number it round 2 and the post's history claims a verdict nobody gave, and `ready <post> 2 …` queues behind a round the queue never opened. Do not read the hold-back as a licence to skip the card forever: the slot has to move, or the queue stalls behind one broken post.

**A partial failure is the same run with the passing slots held, not a card with holes.** When one slot of the post fails the gate and its siblings pass, nothing is shown yet either: submit the pair for the failing slot only, leave the passing draft PNGs untouched and unshown, and open **one** card at the collect wake that carries the replacement renders plus the passing draft images as their own items. Those items point at the vault file the draft already embeds, so approving one is the back-fill case — no vault copy and no new record. Showing the passing slots early splits one round across two verdict cards and asks him to judge half a set; shipping a card with a hole in it is worse, because the round then reads as finished while a slot is still rendering.

## Pass 1 — the prompt pass, before any pixels

Most of the defects are visible in the prompt file and need no read at all. Grep every live record's `## Prompt` for the triggers below. A hit is a broken slot; a prompt carrying three hits is not in doubt.

| trigger | what it renders as | fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `color_palette` array | flat swatch strip at the top or bottom edge, sometimes mid-frame | delete the array, rebuild the look from `aesthetics` / `lighting` / `art_style` in prose |
| literal marks — "a red X drawn over it", "a green checkmark on it", "a tick" | letter-shaped glyphs stamped on the scene | delete the mark; carry the beat by state (bent vs straight, dark lamp vs lit one, cobweb vs spinning fan) |
| a text-inviting surface — clipboard, label, plaque, screen, tablet, board, nameplate, book page, chart | invented words, in a clean sans-serif, pasted over the figure | replace it with a prop whose face has no flat blank plane: a plain crate, a wooden token, a physical tool |
| a bare corner or empty edge band | the model pastes an overlay there | describe a diegetic prop in that region, cropped by the frame edge |
| a set whose members differ by state inside one element | identical members, count wrong | one element per state, each with its own box and its count stated |

A template family is the unit of infection: every image of one draft written in one sitting carries the same array, the same phrasing and the same empty corners, so when one image of a post fails the prompt pass, run the pass over its siblings before writing anything.

## Pass 2 — the sweep, for what a script can settle

`scripts/sweep-assets.py` runs `check-grey-bars.py` and `check-palette-strip.py` over every PNG under `tech_blog/assets/` and writes one JSONL line per asset; `scripts/audit-table.py` joins that to the library records and prints a per-post table. Both run in seconds per image and neither needs a model.

- **Never use `check-flat-bands.py` as a verdict.** It fires on every cel-shaded frame, including approved ones (#66). It is a prompt to read a crop.
- **A contact sheet is settleable by counting gutters: `scripts/check-panel-gutters.py <png>`.** A render that came back as several panels passes every margin probe — the content bbox is the whole frame and the edges carry no bar — and a full-frame read only says "comic-style" in the same words it would use for one drawn scene. The gutter is the hard tell: two runs of consecutive flat rows/columns in one fill, both axes, inside the frame. An 11 px navy gutter grid is a 2×2 contact sheet; a run on one axis alone is usually a painted table edge or floor line, and the script says so instead of calling it. Two frames of one post were settled this way in one pass while the reads disagreed: the header fired on both axes at y402-412 and x843-853, the sibling came back clean, and the clean one was the single-scene picture the halves confirmed. **Do not reimplement this in scratch** — an exact-colour run test splits a grain-dithered gutter into one-pixel runs and reports a contact sheet as a clean frame, which is why the probe joins lines with a colour tolerance. **Both axes firing means "not one scene", and that is two different defects under one signature:** a 2×2 contact sheet with thick dark gutters, and a comic page of bordered panels floating on a pale canvas with white gutters. The probe cannot tell them apart and does not need to — both are held back — but the read that follows names which one it was, because the closing comment says what the frame actually was. One axis alone clears nothing; no run on either axis is positive clearance for a single continuous scene, and a half-frame split is what confirms it.
- **The join needs both prefixes stripped.** A record's `asset:` is `tech_blog/assets/<date>/<file>.png`, the sweep keys are relative to `tech_blog/assets/`, so stripping only `tech_blog/` matches nothing at all. `scripts/audit-table.py` handles it.
- **Expect assets with no record** — a handful of older files predate the library. Report the count rather than silently dropping them.
- **The sweep is not the audit.** It settles margins and edge bands, and the two probes below settle lettering and a flat pasted block; it says nothing about an extra arm, a fused hand or a beat pointing backwards. Say which pass a number came from wherever it is quoted, or the reader takes it for the whole result.
- **Lettering is settleable with no model in the loop: `tesseract <png> - --psm 11`.** Run it on the frame as it sits in the vault and its output is the evidence — a pasted name card comes back as a name plus two non-words, a banner as its sentence, and either string is the unreadable-text or pasted-panel clause settled in one command. Run it on the native file: a 3x nearest-neighbour upscale cut for a crop read returned nothing on a frame whose native file read words cleanly, so an upscaled crop is the wrong input for OCR. Expect the output to be noisy on painted scenes — the finding is the words it does spell, not the garbage it also returns. **A model read that answers "no image attached" has read nothing** — the pixels never reached the reader — so it is never a reason to write a check note out of the prompt sitting in front of you, and it is not a finding about the picture either. **Send one read per turn.** Reads issued several at a time came back blind on all but the last one every time it was tried (four in one turn, only the fourth saw the frame; two in one turn, only the second, twice over), so a crop whose reply says it was not attached is the cheapest thing to re-ask alone. Reach for OCR or `scripts/vread.py` when a read that did see the pixels disagrees with the numbers, or when the alone-retry also comes back blind — do not keep re-asking the same call. **`scripts/vread.py` answers from a reasoning model, so a long multi-part question can spend its whole output budget before it writes a word:** it returns `finish_reason=length, no content returned`, which is a read that never happened, not a clean picture. Ask one short question per call and take one line per answer ("is the key hanging on a hook, or held beside the rail?"), and crop to the prop first when the claim is small — a four-part question on a 2.2x crop failed twice before the single-question version settled it in one line. **And that reader never settles a count:** asked how many keys hang on one rail it answered six on the full frame and five on a 2x crop of the same row, so a number that decides a card goes to `crop-probe.py` or stays out of the note — write "a row of brass keys with the empty hook he points at" and let the beat carry the picture.
- **A pasted block mid-frame is settleable by measuring its own fill colour.** Mask the candidate colour, then print the pixel count, the bounding box and the dense core (the rows and columns above about 80% of the mask's own maximum). A pasted block reads as tens of thousands of pixels inside one axis-aligned box; the same colour filling one whole edge as a solid field is a background plus a blank strip, which belongs to the `check-blank-margin.py` clause instead. Measure a suspect colour against an approved frame of the same series before calling it a defect — some saturated colour is normal in this style, so the count means nothing on its own, and the two green blocks on one frame were indistinguishable by hue from a painted lamp until each was counted separately.
- **After a repair, the frame the repair replaces is the better control than an approved sibling.** The sibling tells you what normal looks like in this style; the rejected frame proves the class is gone, because it is the one picture known to carry that defect. Run `check-defect-signature.py <new render> <rejected frame>` and quote both numbers in the check note: a header whose pasted blue banner measured 57,295 blue pixels came back at 0 on its repair, which is stronger clearance than any read of the new file alone — the file only has to look clean, the pair has to show the signature moved.
- **Not every dark rectangle is a pasted panel, and over-calling one costs a render.** A diegetic opening — a stripped case's bare front, a machine mouth, a hearth — can come back as a dark recessed panel with a bevel, rivets and shading that follows the scene's light, and a full-frame read will describe it as "an old monitor face" and tempt you into a hold-back. Paste has crisp axis-aligned edges, uniform fill and no contact shadow; a recess has depth at its rim. Settle it with the signature probe at zero against the rejected frame plus a 3x crop of the object's own rim, then ship it with the drift named in the item description ("the crate's front reads as a blank recessed panel") instead of holding a clean render back.

## Pass 3 — the read pass, chunked across subagents

The classes the scripts cannot see need eyes on the pixels: pasted panels with invented words, extra arms, a beat pointing the wrong way, a photographic base layer. For a backlog this is the expensive pass, so chunk it.

- **One child per 4-8 posts**, each with the four-clause gate quoted, the trigger table, and the vault/library paths.
- **Read-only, stated as hard rules**: no `imagegen` calls, no board writes, no vault edits, no queue edits. A child that submits a render floods a shared queue you cannot cancel.
- **A fixed output schema**, one object per image: `record`, `png`, `verdict` (clean|broken), `triggers` from a fixed vocabulary, `why` (what is actually in the picture, one sentence), `replacement` (the physical scene a fixed prompt should ask for). The `replacement` line is what the repair pass consumes; without it the audit is a list of complaints.
- **Children's counts are claims, not facts.** Sum their verdicts from the tables they wrote, not from prose in their summary — a child that reports "68 broken" from its own arithmetic is one off as often as not.

## Repair recipe, per slot

1. Start from the audit's `replacement` line and the section's own nouns — but read that line as a **beat, not a prop list**. It was written by a reader looking at the defect, so it can hand back the same family in a new costume: a screen in place of a screen, a dial face, a literal mark. Translate the prop into a physical state (a screen becomes the thing it was reporting on, a mark becomes the state it stood for, a dial becomes the mechanism at its limit) and say which suggestions you replaced in the comment. `references/prompt-hygiene.md` carries the rule. Do not start from the old prompt either: it is the thing that was wrong. **Do start from a recently approved prompt of a neighbouring post, for the skeleton only** — its `style_description`, its character element and its overlay clause are the parts that carry the series' look, and they vary from post to post only in the scene. Copy those three blocks and write the elements fresh from the beat. That is what keeps six new prompts inside one hop; rebuilding the JSON shape from the schema each time is where a batch run runs out of context before it submits.
2. Drop `color_palette`, every literal mark, and every text-inviting surface.
3. Put a described prop in every corner and along both long edges.
4. State the cast and the limbs in the figure's own element ("one person only, exactly two hands and two arms, nothing raised above his shoulder").
5. One action, one prop acted on, and the link **or the break** in a single element whose box spans both ends. **A break is not written like a connection, and an air gap does not survive being described.** A `desc` naming a stub and a matching socket inside one box comes back joined, and so does one that states the gap twice, names the bench and the wall visible through it, and says nothing spans it — the model reads two aligned ends as the instruction and the gap as decoration, so the picture says the opposite of the section and no card note rescues that. Carry the break as a **member laid aside**: take the connecting part out of the linkage entirely and give it a state somewhere in the frame (the drive chain coiled on the bench between two bare sprockets, both ends on the wood and touching neither), so there is nothing drawn between the ends at all. `references/prompt-hygiene.md` carries the mechanism; a slot whose break inverted twice is a mechanism change, not a third wording.
6. bboxes in `[y1, x1, y2, x2]`; run `scripts/check-bboxes.py` and reword a `desc` that contradicts its own box rather than shrinking the box. **A wide box fires on the words `left` or `right` anywhere in its `desc`, not only on a "from edge to edge" phrasing:** a rail written "from the left edge to the right side of the picture" printed `desc says LEFT` and `desc says RIGHT` against the same 880px box, so drop the direction words and describe the extent instead ("spanning the whole width of the wall in one level line") — the prop stays where the scene needs it and the box stays wide. **The script settles the axis order and the left/right and band claims, not the vertical one:** an element described as standing on the floor or lying on the bench prints clean with a box in the top third, because nothing in its `desc` repeats the box's own words back at it. After it prints clean, read every printed y-range against that element's scene nouns — floor and bench live in the lower half, wall and shelf in the upper — and correct the box, because the model follows the box and hangs the prop in mid-air. **A size adjective on a corner prop fires it from inside the full-frame `background` element too:** a shelf written as "a narrow wall shelf in the top right corner" prints `desc says NARROW but the box is 1000x1000`, so keep the prop and drop the adjective ("a small wall shelf") rather than shrinking the background box to match one shelf.
7. `lint` to risk 0 by consolidation, not by rewordings — and the cheapest merge is the connection element absorbing the machine it feeds, which satisfies step 5 (one box spanning both ends of the link) and removes a box at the same time. **A machine's state is the vocabulary that trips the term scorer most often in this series:** write a stopped machine as "cold", "quiet", "dark" or "unlit", never "dead" — the death words are flagged as violence and cost risk 2 per hit, while the cold and dark words pass at 0 and draw the same state.
8. Close with the negative clause naming overlay shapes, and re-lint after every edit.

## Render pairs: ideogram plus flux per slot

The owner compares engines, so a repaired slot is rendered twice — the JSON prompt on `--engine ideogram4` (the one that ships) and a natural-language companion on `--engine flux2-klein` (the comparison draft):

```
imagegen submit --prompt-json  prompts/<item>-r2.json --engine ideogram4 --name <item>-r2
imagegen submit --prompt-file  prompts/<item>-r2-flux.txt --engine flux2-klein --name <item>-r2-flux
```

- **Submit the comparison engine first.** FLUX renders in minutes and ideogram4 in about twenty, and the queue is one FIFO worker: sending the flux files before the ideogram JSONs puts the comparison pictures in your hands hours earlier, so the first collection wake already has something to read against while only the main renders are still pending. The order costs nothing, and the reverse order leaves the comparisons sitting behind the mains for the whole night.
- **`imagegen submit` prints the bare job id and nothing else** — call it plainly and read the line, or pass `--json`. Piping it into a JSON reader yields an empty parse that looks exactly like a submit that failed, and re-running that submit pays for a second render under a second ref that no card will ever name. Echo the id per slot as you go and build the comment's job table from those lines, not from memory.
- FLUX takes prose, not JSON, and its text encoder truncates at 512 tokens. **With the drawn-line clause in the opener the working ceiling is about 425 words, not 300.** Twins that came back with the clause, the grain and the whole negative tail intact ran 368–425 words; first drafts at 433–478 lost the tail, and the tail *is* the negative clause and the hand-count sentence. So write the twin long, count it with `wc -w`, and when it overshoots cut 30–50 words out of the middle — merge the beat sentence into the corners sentence, drop a repeated framing phrase, stop naming an object twice — and never cut the closing clause. The old figure of about 300 predates the drawn-line clause and over-trims a twin that would have rendered clean. Same scene, same cast, no `color_palette`, same negative clause. **The flux companion opens with the drawn-line clause, not a grain instruction** — a clean digital twin and a grain-only twin have both been rejected on style while the ideogram was approved; `references/prompt-hygiene.md` carries the clause and the mechanism (an instruction to be uniform is an instruction to be smooth). **The cap bites hardest at the end of the file, because the negative clause and the hand-count sentence are the last thing written:** an over-long flux prompt loses exactly the protection it closes with, and the picture comes back with the overlay, the extra figure or the third hand those final words existed to keep out. Count it before the submit (`wc -w`), and when one is already over, trim to about 300 and resubmit under a new ref instead of waiting on it — then read both and keep whichever passes. The over-long one is a render that was paid for, not a record: it stays in staging unread, and the comment names it so nobody hunts for a missing file.
- Both renders go on **one card**, item id `-- <slot>`, labelled by engine, so he sees the pair side by side and can say which engine he prefers for that slot.
- **The comparison draft is held to the same gate.** A flux draft with a detached prop or an invented word is not shown "because it is only a draft"; it is the same defect he is rejecting.
- **A pair often comes out asymmetric, and the card carries only what passed.** Keep the surviving render on the slot's own item id, give the comparison item `<slot>-flux`, and name the held-back job id with its defect on the card's face and in the comment that links the attachments — a reader who sees one render where the method says two must not read that as a job that went missing. Do not spend renders to restore the symmetry: a slot with one clean picture is a finished slot, and the batch cap belongs to the next batch. **But a failed ideogram holds the whole round's card.** One card per round carries every slot, so when the slot's own main render is the one that failed, the card waits for its silent re-render — publishing the survivors early splits the post across two verdicts and asks the owner to judge half a set.

## The self-check on a repaired slot: settle a limb claim on a crop, and re-aim a crop that misses

A repair that changes the hands is the repair most likely to come back nearly right, and a full-frame read cannot settle it: at 1216×832 a chibi fist and a fused clump of finger-lines read the same, and the same pass returns "slightly distorted" for both. So the last step before a repaired slot reaches a card is a `crop-probe.py` crop of the hand at 4x–6x, and the crop's answer is the one that counts — "four knuckles and a thumb" passes, "a clustered clump of stubby lines with no count" fails and costs one silent re-render.

**A crop that answers "there is no hand in this box" has missed, and that is not clearance.** Hands sit at the bottom of the figure's own box, or just below it on the surface he leans on, so aim the box from the figure's coordinates rather than from where the pose feels like it should be: crop the whole figure at 3x–4x first, read where the hands are, then take the hand at 6x. Several guessed boxes in one round all answered "no hand here", and each negative read like a clean result.

**A missed crop is the only failure mode here that feels like success**, so state in the check note which box you cropped, not only what it said — a note that reads "hand clean at 6x, box x300-520 y440-610" lets a later round see that the box was aimed at the disc.

**The hold-back costs one render, not a card.** A limb defect found this way is fixed by changing the pose or the prop — the hands come off the small prop, or become closed shapes resting on a surface — rather than by rewording the count sentence; `references/figure-and-prop-prompts.md` carries that recipe.

**The prop gets the same crop as the limbs, because a mangled central prop fails the gate as surely as a mangled hand.** Every limb can count clean while the object the picture is about is drawn as wreckage: a ladder asked for as "one rung sawn through" came back with that rung splintered instead of cut, the rung above it out of level, and the upper rungs meeting the rails at angles no ladder has — which says *damaged* rather than the section's beat, so it fails the fourth clause while the first is spotless. So cut a 2x crop of the prop's own structure (the ladder's rungs, the union's threads, the shelf's brackets) and read it as a building question: does every part meet where it should, and is exactly one thing altered? A prop that reads as wreckage is held back and the slot rides the other engine, the same as a fused hand.

**And crop every corner before trusting a clean script run.** A flat icon pasted into a corner — a filled disc with a thin stem, the shape of a bomb or a tag — passes `check-grey-bars.py` and `check-palette-strip.py` because it is neither a margin nor an edge strip, and a full-frame read calls it "a painted object" because at 1216×832 it is small. The corner crop at 3x settles it: crisp uniform fill, no shading, no contact shadow, and no share of the floor's perspective. A corner the prompt described only as "floor" is exactly where it lands, so name an object in every corner even when a figure already stands in that region (a bucket with a folded rag over the rim beside the android), and hold the render back while the icon is there.

**A duplicate-figure claim is settled by splitting the frame, not by asking the whole frame how many people are in it.** A full-frame read of a picture with one intended man came back as "two characters who look identical, passing a hard hat between them", and a second full-frame pass would only have produced a tie. Write the left and right halves to disk at 2x and ask each half one scoped question — is there a human figure in this crop, and what is it doing — and the count is the two answers added. The halves showed one figure each, so the frame held two: that is the duplicate-body defect, not a quirk to note. An extra body outranks every other defect, so settle it on the halves rather than on the frame's overall impression.

**The over-count direction bites hands too, and the count is read per region, not summed blind.** A full-figure read of a clean two-handed render reported three hands and named an "extra hand tucked behind his back"; a crop of the prop-contact region found two (the fist at the machine plus the resting hand carried in by the box overlap) and a crop of the resting surface found one, so the frame held two and the extra was the overlap. Crop where the hands **must** be — the prop being acted on, and the surface he leans on — read each region on its own, and subtract the region the boxes share before you call a third hand. A whole-figure hand count is the one claim a read invents in the safe direction (it over-calls rather than missing), so it never justifies a hold-back on its own.

## Batch sizing against a shared queue

The renderer is one FIFO worker shared by every run and every card, so the constraint on a batch is the queue, not the plan.

- Read `queue_ahead` from `imagegen status` before submitting. On a deep queue the jobs behind other runs' submissions land hours later, and `eta_minutes` already includes them. **Track the ahead-count, not the ETA:** the ETA is re-estimated from the current depth on every check, so on a busy queue it slides forward by about the wall time since the last one and reads like a render that has stopped moving, while `queue_ahead` is the number that actually falls. Quote the ahead-count in each reschedule comment; it is the only figure that says the worker is progressing.
- **A held queue slot says nothing about the worker, so ask the worker.** The slot blocks a *card*, not a renderer: when the slot's post has its renders finished and its card with the owner, the Max sits idle, and a batch submitted then runs from the front, keeps its ETA and starves nobody. One call answers it:

```bash
ssh m1max 'cd ~/git/ideogram4-local && date -u; sqlite3 jobs.db "select status,count(*) from jobs group by status"; sqlite3 jobs.db "select id,engine,created_at,finished_at from jobs order by created_at desc limit 3"'
```

  Only `done`/`failed` rows with a newest `finished_at` hours old means empty. **Take the clock in that same call**, because `jobs.db` stamps are the Mac's local time (CET = UTC+1) and a stamp read as UTC quotes an ETA an hour late; derive absolute times from `eta_minutes` plus `date -u`, and when a wrong one has already been published to the owner, correct it in a short comment the same run rather than leaving a monitor to fire an hour after the renders finished.
- **Cap a batch at about six renders.** Three slots × two engines is one batch. Ten renders is most of a night and starves every other card's re-render. **Count a batch in slots, never in posts:** when every slot of a post fails the gate, six renders buys one post's three slots, so a method that defines a batch as four posts is unreachable on broken material. Say the batch is one post in the comment, name the next post as the following batch, and if the pace is too slow say the lever is the render cap rather than quietly promising four and delivering one. **A slot whose beat inverted may cost one render beyond the cap, and that is the right trade:** a mechanism change that finally reads is worth more than the sixth render's budget, so spend it and say plainly in the comment that it went over and why, rather than shipping a picture whose story runs backwards or holding the whole card for the next night.
- Monitor on the **last** job's ETA plus five minutes, never the first — for one engine's batch. **A pair batch is the exception, because flux finishes about four times sooner:** three flux jobs at 66-72 minutes and three ideograms at 93-135 in one queue mean that a single hop at the last ETA fetches nothing for two hours while the comparison renders sit finished and uncollected, and the collected set arrives a whole wake late. Arm the first hop just past the *earliest* engine's ETA (the flux trio), collect and check what is done, then re-arm from the ideograms' fresh ETA. **A check more than about three hours out is dropped silently, so store a 2-hour hop and re-arm on every wake.** As the job's own ETA comes inside the hop, shorten the next check to just past that ETA rather than another full two hours; when the job reads `running`, the next check is about 25 minutes out, which is one render on the worker. A hop that finds nothing `done` ends after re-arming, and it posts no issue comment: the monitor is a poll, not a promise to the reader, the numbers belong in the post's queue note where the next run reads them as data, and a hop comment on the thread only restates an ETA that the next wake will slide again. Comment when a render actually arrived, when a check failed, or when the plan changed — never to announce that nothing happened. **Keep the re-arm body's `notes` under 500 characters:** an over-long one answers `400 Validation error` on the `notes` path alone and the whole PATCH lands nothing — the `status` riding in the same body included — so the hop plan goes in the issue comment and `notes` carries the job ids plus the one action the wake takes (`references/paperclip-api.md` has the field list).
- On a deep queue the first wake is usually a reschedule, not a collect: re-read every job, and if none is `done`, PATCH the monitor from the fresh ETA and end the run. That is the monitor working.
- A wake that submits anything must re-arm the monitor itself, or the renders sit finished on the Max with nobody scheduled to collect them.
- A batch that filled its slots is finished — do not chain the next batch inside the same run; write the next batch list into the campaign issue and end.
- **A collection wake that is waiting on renders is the run with spare capacity, so it writes the next batch's prompts.** The renders are already queued, so nothing is gained by idling: write the next batch's JSON and flux files, `lint` each to risk 0, `check-bboxes` each to no contradiction, and record the file paths plus any beat you changed in the waiting post's queue note. Submit nothing — the render cap belongs to the batch in flight — and say in the comment that the prompts are written and unsent, so a later reader does not hunt for job ids that were never created. The next batch then costs six `imagegen submit` calls instead of a whole run.

## Where the work lives

- One campaign issue under the runner carries the gate, the method, the batch cap and the next-batch list; the queue's own state stays in `queue.json`.
- The audit tables are files under `~/image-studio/backlog/audit/` (per-batch markdown, `post | image | verdict | triggers | why | replacement`), because a comment cannot be re-read by the next run as data.
- The campaign comment carries a digest and the file paths, not seventy rows.

## Queue bookkeeping when a repaired post gets its card

A repaired post was taken out of the queue when its broken card was retired: the batch entry was dropped and the post left in `rendering`. Putting it back is a four-call sequence, and each call refuses if the one before it was skipped.

```bash
backlog.py ready <post> <round> backlog/briefs/<post>-round-<round>.md   # queue the round from its brief
backlog.py batch-open-ready 1                                          # turn the queued round into a batch entry
# POST the card issue with that brief as its description, then:
backlog.py batch-opened <post> <IDENT>                                 # record the issue id on the entry
```

- **Write the brief so the card's own run can finish without you.** The card belongs to its own run from the moment it is created, so the verdict interaction is its job and the uploads are usually its job too. The brief carries the absolute staging paths, each file's byte size and sha256, and the per-item id, why and check — that is what a hand-off is, and a brief that only says "the renders are in staging" leaves that run to re-derive the checks you already did.
- **Read `checkoutRunId` on the create response before attempting any upload, and skip the upload when it names another run.** The harness claims a freshly created card within seconds, so the creating run usually finds the issue already `in_progress` under a different run: an upload from the creator then fails on ownership and costs a round trip for nothing, while the card's own run uploads the same files from the paths in the brief. The creator still owes the card one plain comment carrying the per-item departures you would otherwise have put on the card — the key in the air rather than on the hook, the rail carrying fewer keys than asked — because that comment is what stops the card's run re-rendering a render you already passed.
- **The run that creates the repaired post's batch card does not call `card-done`.** The batch entry belongs to the post until its last card closes; releasing the post here marks it done before a single verdict exists, and the open card is then an orphan the queue no longer tracks.
- **Check the description landed byte-identical** on the create response before recording the id: a truncated description silently changes what the card reviews.
- **A plain comment still posts on the new card**, so leave one saying the files are rendered, listed in the description, and that no render or re-audit is owed. It is the cheapest insurance against that run starting over.
- **A batch entry that records no issue while the post records one is queue drift, not a bound card.** The post's `issues` list is written by the create path and the batch entry by `batch-opened`, so a run interrupted between the two leaves `batch[].issue: null` beside a post that names its card. Report it as a row in the runner comment — the card itself is fine and its own run works it — and do not edit `queue.json` or re-run the create to close the gap.
- **`batch-open-ready` opens the oldest queued round, not yours.** The `ready` list is shared by every post whose re-render round was queued, so `batch-open-ready 1` after your own `ready` call can hand back a different post's entry — this session it opened another post's round and left mine still queued. Read the `opened` array in the response and call it again for the remaining count until your post appears; only then create the card. Creating the issue first and recording it afterwards is how a card ends up bound to a post whose brief it does not carry.

## Recording a card on the right slot

`backlog.py opened <n> <IDENT>` does not record an issue to a post; it records one to the **live slot**, and it refuses with `the slot is not held by post <n>` whenever the slot is empty or held by a different post. Two calls can set the slot, and which one to use depends on whether a card is already in flight:

- **Nothing in flight** — `backlog.py next` claims the next `todo` post as the slot's card and prints the brief and title it expects; then `opened <n> <IDENT>` binds the issue id to that slot. Running `opened` without `next` first is the failed call, not a wrong issue id.
- **An earlier post's card is still open** — the slot stays with that post, and the new card runs beside it: `backlog.py batch-open 1` claims the next `todo` post as a batch entry, then `batch-opened <n> <IDENT>` records the issue. Read the `opened` array in the response to confirm it returned the post you carded, because `batch-open` takes the oldest `todo` and not the one you have in mind.

Record the card in the same run that creates it. A post with an issue on the board and no entry in `queue.json` still reads as `todo` to every other runner, and two runners carding one post is the collision the single-slot queue exists to prevent.

## Reporting a total

**Count it with a command, not in your head.** A total in a comment is frozen the moment it posts: `grep -c '^| [0-9]' table.md` and `grep -c '| broken |'` give the number the table actually holds, and the per-file counts let a reader re-derive it. When the posted number is wrong, do not leave it — post a short correction that names the walk-through ("22 rows 20 broken, 19 rows 18 broken, …") and says nothing else changed. An arithmetic slip in a delivery is cheap to correct and expensive to leave, because the next run trusts it.

## Pitfalls

- **A card whose images you have audited and proved broken is retired, even when another run holds it — but only those cards.** The standing instruction is that no card carries an image that fails the bar, so a card showing an audited-broken picture goes: withdraw its pending interaction, then `PATCH` it to `cancelled` with a comment naming the defect and the job ids that replace it. That is not the same as emptying the board: retire the cards whose pictures you have actually audited and whose replacement is queued or scheduled, in batch order, and leave every other card to its own run. What makes this read as forbidden is a refused write — a `403` or `interaction_run_attribution_required` here is the wrong principal, not a boundary, and the retry with your own key succeeds (`references/verdict-and-round-mechanics.md`).
- **An approved image can carry a trigger.** A sibling with the same `color_palette` may be the clean one; measure the strip instead of inferring it (see `references/prompt-hygiene.md`).
- **A picture can be photographic.** If the base layer has depth-of-field blur and real specular light with vector stickers on top, it is not the series at all — engine-pitfall #73.
- **The old prompt is evidence, not a draft.** Its composition may be worth keeping and its wording never is; rebuild the scene from the audit's replacement line plus the section, not by editing the file that produced the defect.
