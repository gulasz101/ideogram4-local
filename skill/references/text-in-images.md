# Text in a render: what the engine actually does

Evidence: post 116 ("Backing up every Hermes conversation to Obsidian"), round 2, three slots. Every prompt asked for specific readable text and every render came back with invented gibberish — `Aesthetic Lataskmipt`, `Spcycrady Weight`, `First auccessful run`, `Archiing thousanos`, `shate.ds`, `sclipsis` — plus floating UI cards in two of the three. The user's rule for that post was "if you are using text, it must be plain english perfectly readable".

## The rule

A requested writing surface is a request for gibberish. Asking for "neat stacks of white paper sheets with text lines", "a display screen showing 4558", "note cards with User and Assistant headings", or "a table with readable column headers" produces lettering that reads as text at thumbnail size and as nonsense at full size. Naming a ban in the same prompt ("no gibberish", "otherwise no text at all") does not hold: the surface draws the words.

So decide per slot which it is:

- **A few short labels only.** Four drawer labels (`default`, `andrzej`, `florian`, `bill-cipher`) and one `state.db` came back correct on the same prompt that produced `shate.ds` on a fifth barrel. The count and length of the strings is what the model can hold; a repeating prop with one label per copy multiplies the failure.
- **No lettering at all** when the meaning survives without it. The header reads as "archiving into a vault" from scrolls, a press and a crystal; the sample-output reads as "the run is done, the shelf is full" from a held-up codex and an android shelving the last volume. Neither needs a character of text, and both were rewritten that way after two failures each.

## What to strip when rewriting

1. Any screen, display, monitor, panel or dashboard — the model paints cards with rows of fake UI text.
2. Paper that "carries text lines" — say *blank* sheets, or pages that show only a margin rule drawn in ink.
3. A count, a title, a date or a filename rendered as lettering — move the fact into the action (a full shelf, a trophy pose) or drop it.
4. `color_palette` — it adds a swatch bar the user has rejected before, and it does not help.
5. Storage boxes, crates, spines, folders "with labels" — say the surface is plain, and describe the colour band instead of the writing.
6. Speech bubbles, thought bubbles, captions, floating text, question marks, exclamation marks, symbols and floating icons (hourglass, gear, star, sparkle) — **a ban does not clear these.** A prompt that stripped every writing surface *and* banned all of them by name still came back with a `??` bubble beside the character's head and three floating icons in the same frame. The bubble is generated by the **face**: the model hangs a bubble next to any visible face it draws, whatever the clause says. Take the face out of the shot — the figure seen from behind, or hands only entering the frame — and the bubble has nowhere to hang.
7. Framed posters, wall art, signs, pinned papers and documents on the wall — **a ban does not clear these either.** A wall described as plain wood panelling with nothing hung on it came back with four pinned sheets carrying squiggle "lines". The pinned paper is generated by the **flat wall facing the camera**: any large blank frontal plane reads as somewhere to hang paper. So do not put one in the shot — shoot across a bench into a shallow dark background with only the hinted edges of shelving, or go overhead so the bench top is the whole frame. Describing the wall as plain, and banning posters and pinned papers, is a hedge; removing the frontal plane is the fix.
8. A flat face on any prop — a "blank" surface still draws a label face; describe the surface as solid colour with grain or texture, no face detail that could carry text (see `references/archive/re-render-defect-persistence.md` §1). When a prop carries a canonical word (a stop sign, a labelled box, a titled book), naming the prop by its usual name pulls that word back even after a "no lettering" ban: a "stop sign with no lettering" still comes back reading STOP. Describe the prop as the blank solid shape instead — a solid red octagon, a solid coloured cube — and drop the prop name that implies the text.

Then say the ban in the positive and name every class: "every surface is plain: no writing on paper, no labels on machines, no signage on the walls, no speech or thought bubbles, no captions, no floating text, no question marks, no symbols, no floating icons, no framed posters, no signs, no pinned papers or documents, no flat label faces". That clause is worth writing — it reads as a description of a plain frame — but for classes 6 and 7 it is a hedge, not a fix. **Those two are drawn by the composition: the face draws the bubble, the frontal wall draws the pinned paper. Never spend a second render rewording the ban against them; change the framing in the same pass you submit**, because a face and a wall cannot be talked out of their furniture, and each round spent on wording is a render cycle behind a shared queue.

**A framing change is worth two compositions in one submit pass, not one.** Both are inside the per-round render cap, and on a deep queue a single hedged variant costs a second wait when it misses. Send two genuinely different scenes of the same beat — for example an eye-level view across the bench with the figure seen from behind, and an overhead flat-lay with hands only entering the frame — then keep the one whose version of the beat's key object is fully in frame and whose cast carries the action. Judge the pair on that, not on which arrived first: the tighter scene can crop the destination object at the edge while the flatter one holds it whole.

## The check

A vision transcription is the only probe: ask for every string one per line and judge each word, and ask specifically whether any bubble, caption, floating text, pinned paper, document or floating icon is present — a frame can be clean of written words and still carry a bubble with `??`, a pinned sheet with squiggles or an hourglass/gear/exclamation icon in it. Reading the frame for "any text?" misses all of them. A slot whose only lettering is four short labels passes; a slot with one invented word or one bubble or one floating icon does not.
